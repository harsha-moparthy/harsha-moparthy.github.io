Some blogs are feeds; a rare few are *bodies of work* — archives you can read end to end and come
out changed. This is that list, with what each one is for.

## In a category of their own

- **[Bartosz Ciechanowski](https://ciechanow.ski/)** — interactive essays (mechanical watch, GPS,
  sound, internal combustion) where every diagram is a live simulation you can drag. Arguably the
  best explanatory writing on the internet, full stop. Read
  [*Mechanical Watch*](https://ciechanow.ski/mechanical-watch/) first and budget an hour.
- **[Dan Luu](https://danluu.com/)** — plain-text posts backed by unreasonable amounts of
  evidence: latency measured across decades of keyboards, why files are hard, how big companies
  actually fail at basics. The signature move is *measuring the thing everyone assumed*.
- **[Gwern](https://gwern.net/)** — long-form research essays with obsessive sourcing (the
  scaling-hypothesis essay aged famously well). Read with the footnotes; that's where it lives.

## Systems and war stories

- **[rachelbythebay](https://rachelbythebay.com/w/)** — two decades of production war stories
  from big-fleet operations, told with names filed off: cascading failures, cron disasters, the
  human factors behind outages. The closest thing our field has to an oral tradition of
  operations.
- **[fasterthanli.me](https://fasterthanli.me/)** — Amos. Rust and systems deep dives that
  refuse to stop at the first layer: "what happens when you run an executable" across multiple
  long parts, networking from raw packets. The persona is playful; the depth is serious.
- **[Fabien Sanglard](https://fabiensanglard.net/)** — source-code archaeology: the Doom and
  Quake *Game Engine Black Books* (free), CPU history through the lens of shipped games. Reading
  other people's code as literature.
- **[Bert Hubert](https://berthub.eu/articles/)** — PowerDNS author. DNS from first principles
  ([Hello DNS](https://powerdns.org/hello-dns/)), the famous
  ["DNA seen through the eyes of a coder"](https://berthub.eu/articles/posts/amazing-dna/), and
  clear-eyed writing on European tech policy from someone who 
ships code.
- **[Geoff Huston / potaroo.net](https://www.potaroo.net/)** and the
  **[APNIC blog](https://blog.apnic.net/)** — where the internet's actual operation is analyzed:
  BGP table growth, DNS behavior at scale, address exhaustion. The macro-view of networking that
  no textbook carries.

## Craft and correctness

- **[Hillel Wayne](https://www.hillelwayne.com/)** — formal methods for working engineers
  ([Learn TLA+](https://learntla.com/) is his), plus the *Crossover Project* interviews on what
  "engineering" means across disciplines. The rare writer who tests folklore instead of
  repeating it.
- **[Matt Might](https://matt.might.net/articles/)** — the CS-education classics: "The Illustrated
  Guide to a PhD," parsing with derivatives, and a drawer full of posts that turn hard topics
  into afternoon reads.
- **[Julia Evans](https://jvns.ca/)** — already on my OS list, but the archive + zines belong on
  any blogroll: the art of being *usefully* confused in public.
- **[antirez](https://antirez.com/)** — Salvatore Sanfilippo, Redis. Design essays from someone
  who thinks in C and writes like an essayist; the posts on simplicity and API design are the
  distilled version of Redis's whole philosophy.

## How to read a body of work

Pick one writer whose domain touches your current project and read their archive oldest-first —
not the greatest hits. Watching the thinking develop across years teaches judgment; isolated
viral posts teach trivia. The pattern across everyone on this list: they measure, they build, and
they write from the artifact — which is the standard this site's projects try to hold.
