A reading list only means something if it's short. These are the books I'd actually re-read, with
what each one is *for*.

## Engineering

- **[Designing Data-Intensive Applications](https://dataintensive.net/)** — Martin Kleppmann. The
  best systems book of its generation; see the distributed-systems post for how to read it.
- **[Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/6515/sicp.zip/index.html)**
  — Abelson & Sussman. Free online. Not about Lisp; about the discipline of building abstractions
  and knowing their cost.
- **[Systems Performance](https://www.brendangregg.com/systems-performance-2nd-edition-book.html)**
  — Brendan Gregg. The reference for how machines actually spend their time.
- **[A Philosophy of Software Design](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/book.php)** — John
  Ousterhout. Short, and the only design book whose advice ("deep modules, simple interfaces,
  define errors out of existence") I find myself quoting in code review.
- **[The Pragmatic Programmer](https://pragprog.com/titles/tpp20/the-pragmatic-programmer-20th-anniversary-edition/)**
  (20th-anniversary edition) — Hunt & Thomas. The craft book; skim annually.

## Science and math

- **[Probabilistic Machine Learning](https://probml.github.io/pml-book/)** — Kevin Murphy. Both
  volumes, free drafts online. The ML reference; see the ML foundations post.
- **[Introduction to Probability](https://projects.iq.harvard.edu/stat110/home)** — Blitzstein &
  Hwang. The probability book that builds intuition without sacrificing rigor.
- **[Surely You're Joking, Mr. Feynman!](https://www.basicbooks.com/titles/richard-p-feynman/surely-youre-joking-mr-feynman/9780393355628/)**
  — the case for curiosity as a working method, disguised as anecdotes.
- **[The Art of Doing Science and Engineering](https://press.stripe.com/the-art-of-doing-science-and-engineering)**
  — Richard Hamming, Stripe Press. "You and Your Research" expanded to a book: how to work on
  things that matter. If you read one book from this list, read this one.

## Judgment

- **[Poor Charlie's Almanack](https://www.stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack)** — Munger's talks;
  free online at Stripe Press.
- **[Thinking, Fast and Slow](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780374533557/thinkingfastandslow)** —
  Kahneman. Read it for the anchor concepts (System 1/2, anchoring, availability), and read it
  knowing some priming studies did not replicate — Kahneman
  [said so himself](https://replicationindex.com/2017/02/02/reconstruction-of-a-train-wreck-how-priming-research-went-of-the-rails/comment-page-1/#comment-1454),
  which is itself a lesson in updating.
- **[The Checklist Manifesto](http://atulgawande.com/book/the-checklist-manifesto/)** — Gawande.
  Why competent people fail at known-solved problems, and the embarrassingly simple fix. Directly
  applicable to deploys, incident response, and reviews.

## How I use the shelf

One book per domain at a time, notes in my own words, and a rule of thumb from Hamming: after
finishing anything good, spend thirty minutes writing down what changed about how I'll work. If
nothing did, the book goes off the shelf — whatever its reputation.
